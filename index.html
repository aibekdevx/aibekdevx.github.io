01 · PaymentsKotlinSpring Boot 3.5PostgreSQLResilience4j

Payments from 7 banks

Customers of a microfinance company repay loans through seven different banks. Before, someone matched bank statements to loan contracts by hand. Now a service pulls payments from each bank, finds the contract and posts the payment to the accounting core on its own. Team of about six. My part: four of the bank connectors, the reliability layer and all of the monitoring. Now about 230 payments a day (≈₸2B a month) go through it, ~96% are matched to a contract automatically, and undelivered payments went from 84 a month to zero.

idempotent intakeone connector per bankno more false alertsretry + rate limit

Details

// polling (cron) or webhook, per bank
BankConnector          ← one class per bank: auth, pagination, statement schema
      ↓
IngestionService       ← normalise, dedupe, resolve contractId
      ↓
PostgreSQL             ← payment_raw · payment · poll_checkpoint · processing_failure
      ↓
CoreDeliveryWorker     ← Resilience4j Retry + RateLimiter → accounting core
      ↓
NightProcessingWorker  ← catch-up for missed days
  • Idempotency in the database. Unique index on (bank_code, bank_tx_id), a repeated ingest just returns the existing row. For banks that do not give a stable transaction id there is a SHA fingerprint of the payload. One bank showed the same payment in the statement and in a settlement registry, so I switched the id to the reference code, which does not change between exports.
  • Parsing the payment purpose. The contract number and national id sit in a text field a person typed: Cyrillic Р instead of Latin P, missing spaces, prefixes, restructuring suffixes. Resolution goes exact match, then LIKE lookup in the warehouse, then fallback by national id.
  • Health checks. I track "did polling run" (last_polled_at) separately from "did the checkpoint move" (since_time). If a bank simply had no payments that day the checkpoint stays put, and the old check reported DOWN. Now there are three states:
UPpolling succeeds, checkpoint within threshold
DEGRADEDpolling succeeds, checkpoint lagging
DOWNno successful poll for longer than the threshold
  • Errors. Transient and business errors are separate, only transient ones get retried, and a transient failure puts the payment back to NEW instead of leaving it half-done. Every failure goes to processing_failure with the stage and a reason code, so incidents are sorted out from a table and not by grepping logs.

Questions I get

Why a unique index and not a SELECT before INSERT?
Two polling runs can overlap, and a webhook can arrive twice. Check-then-insert has a race window. The unique index is the only thing that holds under concurrency, the app catches the violation and returns the existing row.
How likely is a fingerprint collision, and what happens on one?
The fingerprint is a hash of the normalised payload, so two payments would have to match in every field the bank gives us. A match is treated as a duplicate and skipped, but the raw row is still saved, so a false positive shows up in reconciliation and is not lost.
What would you improve first?
Tests for the parsers. The rules for purpose text grew out of real cases and there is no fixture suite for them yet. After that, replace the SSH + supervisor deploy script with the GitLab pipeline the other services already use.
02 · Lending intakeKotlin 1.9Spring Boot 3.2Apache KafkaGatling

Loan applications from 10 dealer marketplaces

Car-loan applications come from ten dealer marketplaces, each with its own API, product list and rates. Underwriting takes minutes, partners want an answer right away. I designed the integration layer and moved the exchange to Kafka: the partner gets an acknowledgement immediately and the decision later, asynchronously.

6 topics · 20 partitionsDLQ per topicnew partner = 2 classescontract PDFs with QR

Details

  • Two interfaces instead of ten if-branches. PartnerStrategy maps an incoming application to our model (product, purpose, rate), PartnerIntegrationService makes the calls back to the partner. A factory picks the implementation by partner name. A new partner is two classes and an enum entry, the core does not change.
  • Kafka. Six topics: new application, status, client registration, client agreement, documents to sign, signed documents with QR. Consumers use MANUAL_IMMEDIATE ack and max.poll.records=1, so a message is committed only after the partner confirmed it got it.
  • Failures. DefaultErrorHandler with backoff and a dlq-<topic> for every topic. Errors that make no sense to retry go straight to the DLQ. Before that one partner with a dead API stopped the whole partition for everyone.
  • Business logic. Rate from a term × down-payment matrix per partner product, annuity payment, validation of blocked products and thresholds per dealer group.
  • Documents. Contracts and print forms from DOCX templates to PDF (PDFBox, POI, XDocReport), signed packages with QR codes, file storage. Also Envers for audit, Caffeine cache for reference data, 18 Flyway versions.
  • Load testing. Gatling simulation for application creation, plus a heap dump to find the hot spot before go-live.

Questions I get

Why Kafka and not HTTP callbacks or RabbitMQ?
The loan pipeline is a separate system and already used Kafka. A durable log lets both sides survive each other's downtime and lets us replay one partner's messages from the DLQ. With HTTP callbacks all the retry state would live inside our service instead of the broker.
What does max.poll.records=1 cost you?
Throughput per consumer. Here the volume is applications per day, not per second, and the call to the partner is the slow part anyway. Confirmed delivery mattered more than batching. If the volume grew I would add partitions and consumers for the busy partners, not globally.
How do you handle a partner sending the same application twice?
Intake is idempotent. A repeated application from the same partner resolves to the existing one and gets its current status back, no second process is started.
03 · Loan originationKotlinSpring Boot 3Camunda BPM 7REST / SOAP

Loan pipeline on Camunda

The full loan process of a retail lender: lead, scoring, underwriting, signing, disbursement, all as BPMN in Camunda. Around 425 Kotlin classes and 130+ Flyway migrations. I moved the credit bureau integration to a new API version without losing applications that were in flight, added the state bureau over SOAP, and built a car-collateral loan product from the data model to the underwriter screens.

retry queue in DBbureau API migrationnew loan productincident debugging

Details

  • Sending statuses to the bureau. Used to be fire-and-forget: a 4xx was ignored and the status marked as sent. I replaced it with a queue table with attempt count, exponential backoff and the stored response body. Failed sends are retried, and when they fail for good you can see why.
  • API migration. New addressing, new required fields, RFC 3339 dates. The bureau turned the old version off earlier than promised. Applications created under the old ids needed a separate update path, which I found and shipped.
  • Car-collateral product. New collateral type in the model and in every outbound integration, loan amount with add-on services, parsing the client's bank statement to prefill payout details, new document categories for the underwriter, BPMN routing that respects auto-approval from scoring.
  • Consistency. An application lives in PostgreSQL, in Camunda and in external systems at the same time. A lot of the work was deciding what runs inside a transaction and what runs as an idempotent call with a retry.

One incident

  • A partner said "we get no answer for this client". The database had nothing for that client at all. The lead was in the logs with a stack trace on a reference lookup: a query that expected one row got two, the dealer directory had duplicate codes. The whole lead was processed in one transaction, so the client, the application and the process were rolled back together, silently. I checked the logs, it was happening every day for eight codes, not one. Fixed both sides: cleaned the data and made the code tolerate duplicates.

Questions I get

Why a queue table and not just a retry annotation?
In-memory retries die with the pod and leave no trace. The table survives restarts, caps the attempts and keeps the bureau's answer, which is what support needs when someone asks why a status was late.
How do you keep 130+ migrations safe across environments?
Before every release we check Flyway version conflicts between branches, because the environments had drifted apart. DDL on busy tables runs with a lock_timeout in a low-traffic window. We learned that after locking a table in prod.
04 · CollectionKotlin 1.3 · JDK 8Spring BootPostgreSQLZabbix

Debt collection service

The collection department works overdue contracts that a service hands out to managers every morning. I cut the nightly batch from ~40 minutes to ~20 (its heaviest step from 23 minutes to 40 seconds, by moving a JPA copy of 4.6M rows into one INSERT…SELECT), took an operator query from 22 minutes to 7 ms, moved the batch out of working hours, built the contract hold feature from schema to REST, and gave ops a job-health endpoint that stopped false alerts. Legacy stack, upgrading was not an option.

batch 2× fasterhold feature with audit logfair distributionno false job alerts

Details

  • Hold feature. A contract can be taken out of collection because of a court case, a complaint or an investigation. The hold sits on the contract, not on the work card, because cards get recreated from the warehouse and the reason has to survive that. It is a flag and a separate tab, not a new status, so the status machine stays as it is and the hold is reversible. The exclusion is in the distribution SQL, not in code filters: there are five distribution paths and one forgotten filter would leak a held contract. The state change and the append-only audit record commit in one transaction, a partial unique index is the last line of defence.
  • Distribution. Round-robin by debt tier with regional segments and per-segment pointers so it stays fair, cumulative SOFT → HARD escalation, segment migrations. Managers can be excluded temporarily (vacation, sick leave) or for good, with a log and roster filtering.
  • Job monitoring for Zabbix. An endpoint with real job durations, a health threshold and a "currently running" guard, so a job that is simply still running is not reported as stuck.
  • Incidents. Cron jobs hanging on a REST client with no timeouts, batch jobs stuck in STARTED, data drift between the warehouse ETL and our ingest, and the table lock from a DDL in prod that started the lock_timeout rule.
  • Prototyped. Near-real-time payments through Kafka: a fast signal from the topic, amounts reconciled against the warehouse.

Questions I get

Why is the hold enforced in SQL rather than in the service layer?
Two reasons. Five code paths hand out contracts, one WHERE in the shared query covers all of them. And PostgreSQL can use the index instead of loading rows just to throw them away.
What did the 60% batch gain actually come from?
First measuring: the job-duration endpoint showed which steps ate the time. Then reworking the slowest steps of the distribution run and the queries behind them, and measuring again.
